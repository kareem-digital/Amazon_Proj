STEP 1

The brief

Every case here starts a new conversation. The agent needs a market, a budget or an impression target, dates, a creative length and a KPI before it will look at inventory.

1.1
CTV campaign in the UK, £15,000, 1 Oct to 31 Oct 2026, 30 second creatives

Reads all four fields, then asks the one thing still missing: Reach or Frequency. Answer Reach and the plan card appears.

Works
1.2
I want to run a CTV campaign → the UK → £15,000 → 30 seconds → 1 Oct to 31 Oct 2026 → reach

One field per turn, in any order. Nothing already given is asked for twice, and the same plan card arrives at the end as in 1.1.

Works
1.3
I need to plan something for TV

No slots at all. The agent says what it does and asks for a market, budget and dates — it does not print an empty form.

Works
1.4
CTV campaign in Sri Lanka, £15,000, 30 seconds

A market VOW does not sell. The agent refuses before asking for anything else, and names the four it does carry. It should never ask for dates on a market it cannot plan.

Works
1.5
CTV campaign in the UK, £15,000, 45 second creatives

45s is not sold. The agent names the lengths that are — 10s, 15s, 20s, 30s. It stops there rather than asking which to use, which is the gap noted below.

Partial

I don't know

Send after a brief with no budget. The agent accepts not knowing and says what it cannot do without it, rather than asking again.

Works

Works
1.8

Works

Works

An impression target instead of a budget. The card shows 5,000,000 impressions and the currency note fires. The turn then stops without offering the inventory — see the gaps.

Works

STEP 3

Budget split

Only appears when the plan holds more than one deal — with one line there is nothing to divide. In the UK at 30s only one deal is bookable, so you have to widen first to reach this step at all.

Walk here first

  1. CTV campaign in the UK, £15,000, 1 Oct to 31 Oct 2026, 30 second creatives
  2. Reach
  3. yes
  4. show me everything — the widened list, where two channels are bookable
3.1
Prime Video and Tubi

Two lines, one deal each — the widened list holds four Prime Video rows and only the 30s one belongs on a 30s plan. The split proposes £7,500 apiece, states which method it used and why, and offers all three answers.

Works
3.2
yes that's fine

Accepts the proposal. Reply is two lines and moves to the audience — it does not restate the split.

Works
3.3
even it out by impressions

The other method. Every line now delivers the same volume and the spend is uneven. The question stays open and now offers even the spend out instead — never the method just applied.

Works
3.4
skip the split

No split. The plan is quoted at a blended CPM and the reply says out loud that nothing is bought at that rate — it is an average across the lines.

Works
3.5

Works against a short list. Against the eleven-row widened list it comes back "not sure what you mean" — "both" of eleven has no meaning, but the reply should say so.

STEP 4

Audience

Three profiles, priced. Declining all three is a fourth answer, not a failure — and it is the one that carries no data fee.

Walk here first

  1. CTV campaign in the UK, £15,000, 1 Oct to 31 Oct 2026, 30 second creatives
  2. Reach
  3. yes
  4. Prime Video
  5. a specific audience — shows the three options
4.1
BALANCED

Named directly. The pick is read back — Balanced audience selected — before the forecast, so the numbers can be attributed to an option.

Works
4.2
the narrow one

Also option 1, second, option 3, broadest. Note the forecast for Narrow and Balanced is the same — at £15,000 the budget binds long before the pool does, which is correct and worth checking you see the same.

Works
4.3
keep it broad

Send at the audience question before the options. No audience set, no data fee — and the reply warns that widening the audience will not be available if reach comes back short. CPM drops to £28.88.

Works
4.4
none of them

The same decision, taken after seeing the three. Must not be read as picking Wide, which is the broadest paid option.

Works
4.5
why is balanced recommended?

Compares all three by pool size and price, and explains that the CPM difference is the data fee rather than the breadth. A question, so the choice stays open underneath.

Works
4.6
actually use the narrow audience

Sent after a forecast. The three come back with the current pick named — You're on Balanced at the moment — so the trader can move between them without starting over.

Works
STEP 5

Location targeting

Optional, and the plan is not delivered until it has an answer either way. Four shapes are supported: a place name, a postcode, a radius around an address, and an exclusion. Watch the reach and frequency move — impressions never do, because the budget buys those wherever the ad runs.

Walk here first

  1. CTV campaign in the UK, £15,000, 1 Oct to 31 Oct 2026, 30 second creatives
  2. Reach
  3. yes
  4. Prime Video
  5. BALANCED — forecast on screen, Step 5 question open
5.1
can we target just London instead of the whole UK?

Resolves to London, England, UK and says it replaces UK-wide. Reach stays ~154k and the reply explains why: 629k of the audience is inside London, and £15,000 only buys enough impressions for 154k of them. The budget is the limit, not the area.

Works
5.2
actually just target SW1A

A postcode. Reach collapses to ~9.7k at frequency 50.8 — the same impressions landing on far fewer people — and the agent advises widening before committing. This is the case where the numbers must visibly differ from 5.1.

Works
5.3
target SW1A, EC2A and N1

A pasted list. SW1A resolves; the mock has no EC2A or N1, so they are reported back as unmatched. Against real VOW data all three would resolve — the agent is doing the right thing with the data it has.

Partial
5.4
within 3km of Manchester city centre

A radius. This mints a new location on the platform, so the agent reads the address and the distance back and waits for a yes before creating anything. Say yes to confirm.

Works
5.5

Miles as well as kilometres, and around as well as within … of. Confirm it and compare the reach against 5.4 — a 10-mile circle is much larger than a 3km one, and the numbers have to show it.

Works

The degenerate case. Read correctly rather than ignored — worth confirming it does not silently forecast the same reach as a real radius.

Works

Works

ANY TIME

Questions

A question must never lose the trader's place. After any of these, whatever the agent was waiting for should still be waiting — check the open question comes back or stays answerable.

Q.1
what CPM are we paying?

Two figures, and the difference named: the deal rate £28.88, and £30.51 as what the plan is actually charged with the audience fee on top. The second line only appears when the two differ.

Works
Q.2
what if my budget was only £5,000?

Uses the figure in the question, not a guess. Scaling table at the plan's effective CPM, and an offer to re-plan at that number. Try what if I doubled the budget? too, which has no figure and gets 0.5× / 1× / 1.5×.

Works
Q.3
is that enough?

Refuses to answer for the trader — it depends on how many people they need — but gives the arithmetic and two larger budgets to compare against.

Works
Q.4
what is a CPM?

Definitions work cold, with no plan at all. Also what is a deal?, what is CTV?, what is ROS?, what's a preferred deal?, what's the difference between reach and impressions?, do I have to use an audience?

Works
Q.5
which markets can I plan in?

Counted live, never quoted from a constant: 21 supported, 6 with inventory. Same for what channels do you have? and what creative lengths can I run?

Works
Q.6
what fees are there?

The audience data fee and the product-category fee, kept apart and fetched from their own sources — they are different fees and both can apply.

Works
Q.7
what's on the plan so far?

Reprints the card mid-flow. Pair it with what else do you need? on a half-finished brief, which lists only the gaps.

Works
Q.8
why is the frequency 3.2?

Shows the division that produced it and names the two levers that bring it down.

Works
Q.9
how much should I spend?

Works off a market and a length, before any budget exists.

Works
Q.10
what's the weather in London?

Genuinely off topic, and refused — but only when nothing is outstanding. Send it while the agent is waiting on an answer and it must not deflect, because that would tell someone who answered the question that their answer was off topic.

Works
Q.11
that reach feels a bit low for 15k

Should offer a repair — a wider audience, a bigger budget. Comes back "that didn't change anything on the plan" instead.

Gap

Known gaps

  • 1.5 — an unsold creative length stops mid-sentence. It names the lengths that are sold and then ends, without asking which to use.
  • 1.10 — an impression target ends the turn early. The card and the currency note arrive, but the offer to look at inventory does not.
  • 2.9 — "which is cheapest" does not rank. It prints the rate card, uses the market code rather than its name, and repeats the "needs curation" label on a channel whose real problem is no deal in this market.
  • 3.5 — "both" against a long list. Meaningful for two or three options, meaningless for eleven; the reply should say which it is rather than failing to parse.
  • 5.3 — the mock only knows some postcodes. SW1A resolves, EC2A and N1 do not. This is fixture data, not agent logic, and should disappear against real VOW.
  • 5.8 — a bare exclusion does not forecast. Recorded and read back, but no numbers follow and Step 5's question does not return.
  • Q.11 — a complaint about the reach is not read as one. "that reach feels low" should open the repair loop and instead reports that nothing changed.
  • 7.3 — holding reads as an unknown question. "not yet" routes correctly but has no reply written for it.
  • 7.4 — naming the campaign mid-flow. The name is not taken and the turn re-lists whatever was last on screen.
  • Steps 9–13 are not built. Creative upload, platform approval, tracking, credit check and activation. Creation is where this flow currently ends, which is why the reply says the strategy is not running yet.
